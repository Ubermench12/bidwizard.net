IThe problem

Capability isn't usually the problem. Bandwidth and compliance are.

Government contractors lose bids for reasons that have nothing to do with whether they can do the work — an incomplete compliance matrix, a technical volume that reads like a brochure, or simply no one on staff whose actual job is proposals.

What usually happens

A generalist proposal writer formats the response correctly but fills it with language generic enough to apply to almost any bidder, because they aren't positioned to evaluate your actual approach.

What Bid Wizard does instead

Build the compliance matrix first, write every volume against your real capabilities, and review the finished proposal against the government's own evaluation criteria before it's submitted.

VQuestions

Before you reach out

Do I need to already be registered in SAM.gov?

No — that's often the starting point. Registration, capability statements, and certification questions are covered under Government Certifications.

What's the difference between GovCon consultation and proposal development?

Consultation happens before a solicitation is released — pipeline research, positioning, teaming. Proposal development starts once the RFP is actually in hand.

Do you work with contractors who have never bid on a government contract before?

Yes — getting a business registered, certified, and positioned for its first submission is a core part of the work, not an edge case.

Do you cover state and local (SLED) opportunities, or only federal?

Both. Federal, state, and local procurement each carry their own rules, and each is treated as its own track rather than a federal afterthought.

Can I bring in just one piece instead of the full package?

Yes. Proposal development, consultation, and certification support are all available as standalone engagements.
